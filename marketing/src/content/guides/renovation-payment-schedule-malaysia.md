A typical renovation payment schedule in Malaysia starts with a **10–20% deposit** on confirmation, followed by
**progress payments tied to finished stages** of work, a payment at handover and a **retention of about 5%** released
after the defects period. Homeowner guides treat a deposit above **30%** as a warning sign, and CIDB advises that
payments follow the percentage of work completed. Write the schedule, the payment terms in days and the retention into
the quotation, so there is nothing to argue about later.

## How much renovation deposit is normal in Malaysia

The deposit secures the client's slot and covers your early costs. Published guides give a fairly consistent range:

| Source | Deposit guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Qanvast (2024) | 10–20%, sometimes up to 30%, usually non-refundable; warns about requests of up to 50% |
| PropertyGuru | 15–20% of the total cost, generally non-refundable |
| Recommend.my (2024) | No more than 30% for bigger projects; never full payment upfront |

The trust problem is real. In 2024 CIDB's chief executive said contractors often disappear after receiving the advance
payment, and that verbal agreements are not enough. A client who has read that will be wary of a large deposit, so a
modest deposit with clear milestones often wins the job faster than a big one.

CIDB's own standard renovation contract treats the advance as an amount the parties write in, with a default of nil if
it's left blank. If you need more upfront for long-lead items, such as custom carpentry or imported tiles, say what the
money buys and put it on its own line.

## Progress payments tied to milestones

After the deposit, tie each payment to a stage the client can see on site. CIDB's advice to homeowners is that payments
should follow the percentage of work completed, and PropertyGuru recommends paying in stages linked to specific
milestones. Good milestones are:

- **Hacking, masonry and wet works completed** (tiling, screeding, waterproofing).
- **Electrical and plumbing first fix completed**, before the ceiling closes.
- **Carpentry installed**, or delivered to site if you agree that in writing.
- **Practical completion and handover**, with a short defects list.

Avoid dates ("payment on 15 March") as milestones. If work slips, you'll be chasing a payment for work that isn't
done, and the client will refuse. Avoid vague stages too ("50% progress"), because nobody can agree what 50% looks like.

Long jobs can use monthly valuations instead: you claim the value of the work done each month. CIDB's standard contract
works this way unless the parties attach a stage payment schedule.

## Sample renovation payment schedule (illustrative)

This is an **illustrative** schedule for an RM80,000 condo renovation. Adjust the stages to the trades in your job.

| Stage | Milestone | % | Amount (RM) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Deposit on signed quotation | 15% | 12,000.00 |
| 2 | Hacking, masonry and wet works completed | 30% | 24,000.00 |
| 3 | Carpentry, electrical and plumbing installed | 30% | 24,000.00 |
| 4 | Practical completion and handover | 20% | 16,000.00 |
| 5 | Retention, released after the defects period | 5% | 4,000.00 |
| | **Total** | **100%** | **80,000.00** |

Check that the percentages add up to 100% and that the amounts add up to the contract sum. When the client approves a
[variation order](/guides/variation-order-template-malaysia), state on the VO which payment it's added to, so the
schedule and the revised contract sum still match.

## Payment terms: how many days to pay

A milestone tells the client *when* a payment falls due. You also need to say *how quickly* they must pay. Write it in
days, for example "payable within 7 days of our invoice".

- **CIDB's Standard Terms of Construction Contract for Renovation and Small Projects** lets the parties fill in the
  period. If it's left blank, the client must pay within **7 days** of each payment certificate.
- The same contract lets the contractor give written notice for an unpaid certificate and **suspend work** if the client
  still hasn't paid **7 days** after receiving that notice.
- Where the **Construction Industry Payment and Adjudication Act 2012 (CIPAA)** applies and the contract has no payment
  terms, the default is monthly progress payments, due **30 calendar days** from receipt of the invoice.

Mah Weng Kwai & Associates advises homeowners to pay promptly when payments are due, and notes that non-payment may lead
a contractor to slow down or stop work. Clear terms protect both sides.

## Retention: hold back a small final sum

Retention is a part of the price the client keeps until defects are fixed. Published figures vary:

| Source | Retention |
| --- | --- |
| CIDB, via NST (2024) | 5–10% of the contract value, paid after the work is complete and the owner is satisfied |
| CIDB standard renovation contract | 10% of each certified amount, capped by default at 5% of the total price |
| Mah Weng Kwai & Associates (2020) | Usually 10%, sometimes more on small contracts |

Under the CIDB contract's defaults, the defects liability period is **12 weeks** from practical completion, and the
retention is released with the final account within **30 days** of the final completion certificate. The contract also
lets the contractor provide performance guarantee insurance instead of retention. If you'd rather not have retention
deducted from every claim, the simpler option for small jobs is a single 5% stage at the end, as in the sample above.

## Does CIPAA apply to renovation work?

CIPAA gives contractors a fast adjudication route for unpaid claims. It covers construction contracts **made in
writing**, and its definition of construction work includes renovation and alteration. Under CIPAA a contractor serves a
written payment claim, and the client has **10 working days** to respond. Clauses that make payment depend on the payer
first being paid by someone else, or on its financing being available ("pay when paid"), are void.

There's an important exclusion in section 3. CIPAA does not apply to a contract entered into by an individual for work
on a building **under four storeys** that is **wholly intended for that person's own occupation**. Many owner-occupied
landed houses fall into that exclusion. In *Liew Piang Voon v WLT Project Management* (2020), as summarised by Donovan &
Ho, the High Court held that the exclusion did not apply where the owner renovated a house for his daughter to run a
kindergarten, because he would not occupy it himself.

Company clients, shops, offices and work on buildings of four storeys or more are more likely to be inside CIPAA. Whether
a particular job is covered depends on the facts, so check with a lawyer before relying on it.

## What to write in the quotation

- **The schedule itself**: stage, milestone, percentage and RM amount, adding up to 100%.
- **Payment terms in days** from your invoice, and how to pay (bank transfer to your company account).
- **Retention**: percentage, and when it's released.
- **Variations**: priced in writing before the work, and which payment they're added to.
- **What happens if a payment is late**: for example, that you may suspend work after written notice.
- **Validity**: how long the price and schedule hold.

CIDB has offered free sample contracts for renovation and small projects since 2015. Using one, or attaching your
quotation to one, gives the client terms they can check against a recognised standard. Show your
[CIDB registration](/guides/cidb-registration-malaysia) number on the same quotation, so they can check you too.
