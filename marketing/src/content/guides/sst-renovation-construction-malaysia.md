Since **1 July 2025**, construction work services in Malaysia are taxed at **6%**, but only contractors **registered
for service tax** charge it. You must register once your taxable construction work exceeds **RM1.5 million in 12
months**, and work on **residential buildings is excluded**. So most small contractors renovating homes don't add
SST, while larger contractors doing shops, offices and other commercial work usually must. Here's how it works and
how to show it on a quotation.

This guide is general information, not tax advice. The rules changed several times in 2025 and 2026, so confirm your
own position with your tax agent or the Royal Malaysian Customs Department (RMCD).

## The rules at a glance

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Tax | Service tax on construction work services (Group L) |
| Rate | 6% |
| From | 1 July 2025 |
| Registration threshold | More than RM1.5 million of taxable construction work services in 12 months |
| Residential buildings | Excluded, including related public facilities |
| Completed residential buildings | Exempt under Service Tax Policy 2/2026 (18 June 2026), backdated to 1 July 2025 |
| Commercial and industrial work | Taxable if you're registered |
| Government projects | Federal, state and local authorities are exempt from paying it, with supporting documents |

## Do you need to charge SST? Three questions

1. **Are you registered for service tax under Group L?** If not, you don't charge it, and you mustn't show SST on your
   quotation or invoice.
2. **Does your taxable work exceed RM1.5 million in 12 months?** If so, you need to register. Only taxable work counts:
   excluded residential work doesn't, as long as your documents show why it's excluded.
3. **Is this job taxable?** For a registered contractor, work on a shop, office, factory or other non-residential
   building is generally taxable. Work on a residential building isn't.

## Renovating homes: the residential exclusion

Construction of residential buildings and related public facilities is outside the tax. In June 2026, RMCD issued
**Service Tax Policy 2/2026**, which exempts construction work on **completed residential buildings**, meaning
renovation of existing homes, with effect from 1 July 2025. Registered contractors who had already charged service
tax on this work could claim a refund, after first refunding their customers.

Keep documents that show the property is residential, and put the client's name and the property address on your
quotation and invoice.

## Mixed developments and shophouses

When one job covers both residential and commercial space, only the **non-residential part is taxable**. Split it
using the contract, drawings and bill of quantities. Where the values can't be separated, a wider amount may end up
being taxed, so itemise from the start.

## Materials vs labour on your invoice

Service tax applies to the **construction work service**. RMCD's guidance allows materials to be listed separately,
at actual cost with supplier invoices to support them, so that tax applies only to the service portion. If your
invoice is a lump sum that doesn't separate goods from services, the whole amount may be taxed.

For a registered contractor on a commercial job, that's a strong reason to quote and invoice by line item, not as one
lump sum.

## How to show SST on a quotation

**If you're not registered** (most small renovation contractors):

- Don't add a service tax line, and don't write "SST included".
- Your total is simply your price.

**If you're registered and the job is taxable:**

- Show your **SST registration number** on your invoices, and ideally on the quotation too.
- Show the **subtotal**, the **service tax at 6%** and the **total including tax** as separate lines.
- If you list materials separately, keep them at cost and keep the supplier invoices.

**If you're registered but the job is residential:**

- Leave the tax at 0%, and keep the documents that show the property is residential.

## Contracts signed before the tax started

Contracts signed before the tax started, with **no price review clause** and stamped by the deadline, are exempt until
**30 June 2027**. From 1 July 2027, work under those contracts becomes taxable. If you have a long-running contract
from before July 2025, check the exact conditions with your tax agent.

## Quotations are not invoices

A quotation isn't submitted to LHDN's MyInvois system, and service tax is accounted for on your invoices, not your
quotes. But showing the tax correctly on the quotation avoids a nasty surprise for the client when the invoice
arrives. For how quotation tools and accounting software split the work, see
[quotation software in Malaysia](/quotation-software-malaysia).
