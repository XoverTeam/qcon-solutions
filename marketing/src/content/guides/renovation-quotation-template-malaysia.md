## What a professional renovation quotation should include

1. **Who you are.** Registered company name, SSM number, address and a contact person. With renovation scams
   in the news, showing you're a registered business is the quickest way to earn trust.
2. **Who and where.** The client's name and the exact site address, including unit number.
3. **Itemised work, grouped by trade.** "Kitchen renovation – RM28,000" invites arguments. "Supply & lay
   600 × 600mm homogeneous floor tiles, 450 sq ft @ RM11" doesn't. Lump-sum quotes with no breakdown are one
   of the first red flags homeowner guides warn about.
4. **Unit, quantity and rate on every line.** This is what lets you re-measure fairly and price variations
   consistently later.
5. **Exclusions.** Say what's *not* included: loose furniture, appliances, light fittings, management deposits, authority fees.
6. **Validity period.** Material prices move. A clear "valid for 30 days" protects you.
7. **A payment schedule tied to milestones**, not dates.
8. **Terms**: how variation orders are handled, the defects liability period, who handles renovation permits,
   and the estimated duration.
9. **Acceptance.** A line for the client to sign, which turns your quotation into an agreement.

## How to price each line without losing margin

Most underquoting doesn't come from bad rates. It comes from **missing lines** and **confusing markup with margin**.

**Lines contractors commonly forget:**

- Floor, lift and corridor protection, plus renovation permit handling with the management
- Debris disposal and lorry trips
- Waterproofing before tiling in wet areas
- Making good after hacking (plastering and skimming)
- Final cleaning before handover
- Wastage allowance on tiles and boards (cut pieces, breakage)

**Markup vs margin.** If an item costs you RM100 and you add a 30% *markup*, you sell at RM130, but your
*margin* is only 23% (RM30 ÷ RM130). To earn a true 30% margin you need:

| | Formula | Selling price on RM100 cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30% markup | cost × 1.30 | RM130.00 |
| 30% margin | cost ÷ (1 − 0.30) | RM142.86 |

On a job that costs you RM50,000, that's the difference between quoting RM65,000 and RM71,429, more than RM6,000. Decide which one you mean, and apply it the same
way on every line.

## Should you add SST to a renovation quotation?

Since **1 July 2025**, construction work has been a taxable service at **6%** in Malaysia. Two conditions
mean most small renovation contractors still don't charge it:

- **Only registered contractors charge it.** Registration is required once your taxable construction services
  exceed **RM1.5 million in 12 months**.
- **Residential buildings are excluded.** Construction work on residential buildings falls outside the tax,
  and Service Tax Policy 2/2026 (issued June 2026) confirms the exemption for work on *completed* residential
  buildings, backdated to 1 July 2025. To rely on it, the contractor must keep proof that the building is
  residential, and the invoice must show the client's name and the property address.

Commercial fit-outs (offices, shops, F&B) are a different story: if you're SST-registered, they're generally
taxable. Leave the template's tax rate at **0%** unless you're registered and the job is taxable. The rules
changed twice in a year, so confirm with your tax agent or the Royal Malaysian Customs Department.

## A payment schedule homeowners will trust

Homeowners in Malaysia are wary of renovation scams, and the deposit is where trust is won or lost.
Homeowner guides describe a **10–20% deposit** as normal and treat **more than 25% upfront** as a red flag.
The template's default schedule follows that:

| Stage | Milestone | % |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Deposit upon confirmation | 20% |
| 2 | Hacking, masonry & wet works completed | 30% |
| 3 | Carpentry, electrical & plumbing installed | 30% |
| 4 | Completion & handover | 15% |
| 5 | Retention, released after the defects period | 5% |

Tie each payment to something the client can *see* on site. That gets you paid faster, and it gets you paid
with fewer arguments.

## Common quotation mistakes

- **No validity date**, so a client comes back six months later expecting the old price.
- **"Any changes, settle later."** Put variation orders in writing with a price before doing the work.
- **Rounding everything into lump sums**, which makes every change a negotiation.
- **Sending the spreadsheet itself.** Export to PDF so the client can't edit your figures.
- **Re-typing old quotations** for similar jobs and missing a line along the way.
