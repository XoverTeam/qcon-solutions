**Markup** is your profit as a percentage of **cost**. **Margin** is your profit as a percentage of the **selling
price**. On an item that costs you RM100, a 30% markup gives a selling price of RM130, but your margin is only
**23.1%**. To earn a true 30% margin you'd need to sell at **RM142.86**. Mixing the two up is one of the quietest
ways contractors underquote. Below are the formulas, two conversion tables and worked examples.

## The formulas

| | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Selling price with a markup | cost × (1 + markup) |
| Selling price for a target margin | cost ÷ (1 − margin) |
| Markup → margin | markup ÷ (1 + markup) |
| Margin → markup | margin ÷ (1 − margin) |
| Margin on a line | (selling price − cost) ÷ selling price |

## Why it matters on a real job

Say a renovation costs you **RM50,000** in materials, labour and subcontractors, and you want a 30% margin to cover
overheads and profit:

| Pricing | Selling price | Profit | Actual margin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 30% markup (cost × 1.30) | RM65,000 | RM15,000 | 23.1% |
| 30% margin (cost ÷ 0.70) | RM71,428.57 | RM21,428.57 | 30.0% |

The gap is **RM6,428.57** on a single job. If your overheads (rent, vehicles, staff, insurance) take 15% of sales,
the 30% markup leaves about 8% profit, while the true 30% margin leaves about 15%.

## Margin by line, not just in total

A quotation's overall margin can look healthy while individual lines lose money. Common culprits:

- **Carpentry** priced from an old supplier rate after board prices went up.
- **Subcontracted trades** (plumbing, aircon, glass) passed through with little or no markup.
- **Small "lot" items** like protection, disposal and cleaning, priced from memory.
- **Discounts** given on the total, which come straight out of margin.

Checking cost and selling price on every line before you send catches these. On an item that costs RM100 and sells for
RM142.86 (a 30% margin), a RM10 discount cuts the margin to about 25%.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Saying "30%" without saying which.** Decide whether your target is markup or margin, and write it down for your team.
- **Adding markup to a discounted total.** Work out the discount's effect on margin before agreeing to it.
- **Forgetting overheads.** Margin has to pay for rent, vehicles, admin time and defects, not just profit.
- **Not updating costs.** A rate that made 30% last year may make 20% today.
