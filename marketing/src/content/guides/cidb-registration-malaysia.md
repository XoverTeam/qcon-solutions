**CIDB registration** is compulsory for renovation contractors in Malaysia: the CIDB Act requires anyone who carries
out construction works, including repair and renovation, to register, and working without it carries a fine of
**RM10,000–100,000**. Most small renovation firms start at **grade G1**, which covers contracts of up to
**RM200,000** each. A new G1 registration costs a **RM50 processing fee** plus a registration fee of **RM20 a year**,
and is valid for up to 2 years.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. CIDB updates its requirements from time to time, so confirm your
own position on the CIDB website or its CIMS portal before you apply.

## Who needs CIDB registration

Section 25 of the Construction Industry Development Board Act 1994 (Act 520, amended in 2011) has required every
contractor, local or foreign, to register with CIDB since 20 July 1995 before taking on construction work. CIDB's
definition of construction works is wide. It covers building, but also repair, maintenance, renewal, alteration,
conversion, renovation and demolition. Hacking a bathroom, rebuilding a kitchen or fitting out a shop all fall inside
it.

The Act lists some exemptions under Section 40(1), but CIDB's registration page doesn't spell them out, and none of
the CIDB documents we reviewed sets a minimum job value below which you don't need to register. If you think you're
exempt, get it confirmed by CIDB in writing.

It matters to your clients too. In 2024 CIDB's chief executive warned that a homeowner who can't show they appointed
a contractor could be treated as the contractor themselves under Section 25. That's one reason careful clients now
ask for your registration number before they sign.

## CIDB grades G1 to G7

Your grade sets the largest contract you may tender for or carry out. Taking a job above your grade's limit, or
outside your registered category, breaks the terms of your registration.

| Grade | Contract value limit | Registration fee (CIDB) | Minimum paid-up capital (reported) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | Up to RM200,000 | RM20 | RM5,000 (RM10,000 with SPKK) |
| G2 | Up to RM500,000 | RM80 | RM25,000 |
| G3 | Up to RM1,000,000 | RM150 | RM50,000 |
| G4 | Up to RM3,000,000 | RM350 | RM150,000 |
| G5 | Up to RM5,000,000 | RM700 | RM250,000 |
| G6 | Up to RM10,000,000 | RM1,000 | RM500,000 |
| G7 | No limit | RM1,400 | RM750,000 |

The contract limits and fees come from CIDB's own documents; its fee schedule lists the registration fees per year.
The paid-up capital figures aren't in the CIDB pages we could read. They come from a 2019 news report of the
government's announcement and from 2026 registration guides, which agree with each other. Check the current figure in
CIMS when you apply.

For a renovation firm, G1 suits single homes and small shops. If you regularly quote whole landed houses or office
fit-outs above RM200,000, plan for G2.

## What CIDB G1 registration involves

You apply online through **CIMS** (cims.cidb.gov.my), and the application is submitted by an owner or director. One
person can apply for only one company.

1. **Register the business with SSM first.** CIDB registers local contractors with at least 70% local equity.
2. **Prepare your technical person.** 2026 registration guides say G1 to G3 need one Malaysian holding a relevant
   technical certificate where available. If you have none, the owner or a director must later pass CIDB's business
   management course (SKP) before the first renewal.
3. **Pay the RM50 processing fee within 7 days** of submitting, or the application is cancelled.
4. **Pay the registration fee within 14 days** of the approval letter, or the approval lapses. Fees aren't refunded.
5. **Print your certificate (PPK) online.** There's no counter collection.

New contractors start with two default specialisations: **B04** (general building) and **CE21** (civil engineering).
After registering, one owner or director must attend the **Integrity and Code of Ethics course** within 2 years, or
before the next renewal if that comes first. CIDB may also ask a G1 contractor to hold an **MCORE** rating before the
next renewal.

If you want government work, you also need the **SPKK** certificate, which has no registration fee but needs a valid
PPK. Bumiputera firms can apply for the **STB** status certificate in the same system.

## Specialisation codes for renovation work

Beyond B04, CIDB adds specialisations when you show a letter of award for that kind of work or a CIDB skills
certificate. A 2026 list of CIDB codes includes these that renovation contractors and interior designers use most:

| Code | Work |
| --- | --- |
| B04 | General building construction |
| B07 | Interior decoration, including ceilings and partitions |
| B08 | Waterproofing |
| B13 | Tiling |
| B14 | Painting |
| B18 | Metal works, such as grilles and awnings |
| B26 | Demolition |
| B28 | Renovation: additions and alterations to buildings |
| E04 | Low-voltage electrical installation and wiring |

Some codes need another licence as well. Electrical wiring needs registration with the Energy Commission, and
internal plumbing (B10) needs a permit from the water regulator, SPAN. Each change to your codes costs a RM30 processing fee in CIMS.

## Keeping your registration valid

- **Renew before expiry.** Renewal costs a RM30 processing fee plus 50% of your grade's registration fee if your
  grade stays the same. The renewal period is 1 to 3 years, depending on your CCD points.
- **Collect CCD points.** CIDB requires registered contractors to take part in its Continuous Contractor Development
  programme. The points held by owners, directors and staff decide how long your next registration runs.
- **Stay active.** A contractor with no activity for 3 years in a row can have the certificate revoked.
- **Declare projects.** Submit project details to CIDB within 14 days of being awarded a job.
- **Show your number on site.** Display your CIDB registration number on a signboard at each site, and make sure
  site workers hold a valid Construction Personnel Card (the CIDB "green card").

## CIDB on your quotation

Your registration is part of what a client checks before signing, so put it where they can see it:

- **Show your CIDB registration number and grade** next to your SSM number on every quotation. Clients can look it
  up on the CIMS portal.
- **Check the grade limit against the contract sum**, including likely variations, before you quote a large job.
- **Mention the CIDB levy on big contracts.** CIDB charges a levy of **0.125%** on construction contracts above
  **RM500,000**, settled by the main contractor who declares the project. On a RM600,000 contract, for example, the
  levy is RM750 (illustrative). Most G1 jobs are below the threshold.
- **Use a recognised contract.** CIDB's free Standard Terms of Construction Contract for Renovation Work and Small
  Projects has no minimum or maximum value, and pairs well with an itemised quotation.
