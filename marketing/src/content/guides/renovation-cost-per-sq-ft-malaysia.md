In 2026, renovation in Malaysia costs roughly **RM40–120 per sq ft for a light refresh**, **RM90–250 per sq ft
for a standard mid-range renovation**, and **RM170–450+ per sq ft for a premium or full overhaul**. A typical
1,000 sq ft condo renovation lands around **RM80,000–200,000**. Below are the published ranges, the rates by
trade behind them, and a worked example that shows how a per-sq-ft figure is built up line by line.

## Renovation cost per sq ft: what 2026 guides say

Every source defines its tiers a little differently, which is why the ranges overlap. Here they are side by side:

| Source (2026) | Light refresh | Mid-range | Premium / full overhaul |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| iHome.my | RM50–120 | RM120–250 | RM250–450+ |
| ClickBina | RM50–90 | RM90–150 | RM150–220+ (structural RM180–300+) |
| Klang Valley report cited by Duotone Studio | RM40–70 | RM90–160 | RM170–300+ |

What usually separates the tiers:

- **Light refresh**: repainting, new flooring over existing tiles, minor repairs, new lights. Little or no hacking.
- **Mid-range**: new kitchen and bathrooms, built-in carpentry, plaster ceiling and electrical work.
- **Premium / full overhaul**: hacking walls, moving plumbing, premium finishes, extensive custom carpentry, and layout changes on landed homes.

The per-sq-ft figure is based on the **built-up area** of the home, not just the floor area you work on.

## Renovation rates by trade

These are the published 2026 rates that a per-sq-ft figure is made of. Treat them as a reference to check your own
rates against, not as prices to copy. Location, access, quantity and material grade all move them.

| Trade | Typical published rate (2026) | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hacking / demolition | RM2,000–3,000 | per area (e.g. one bathroom) |
| Rebuilding walls | RM50–80 | per sq ft |
| Waterproofing, bathroom wet works | RM1,500–2,000 | per bathroom |
| Tiling labour (tiles not included) | RM10–30 | per sq ft |
| Ceramic / porcelain tiles | RM11–15 | per sq ft |
| Plaster ceiling | RM6–15 | per sq ft |
| Electrical, lighting point | RM65–90 | per point |
| Electrical, 13A socket outlet | RM100–150 | per point |
| Full rewiring | RM3,500–15,000 | per home |
| Interior painting | RM1.50–6.80 | per sq ft |
| Kitchen cabinets, melamine | RM180–300 | per ft run |
| Kitchen cabinets, plywood | RM330–600 | per ft run |

Bathrooms are priced per bathroom more often than per sq ft; see the [bathroom renovation quotation guide](/guides/bathroom-renovation-quotation-malaysia). Plaster ceilings mix units: the flat ceiling per sq ft, L-boxes and cornice per ft run (see the [plaster ceiling price guide](/guides/plaster-ceiling-price-malaysia)). Electrical work is priced per point, with the DB box as its own line (see the [electrical wiring quotation guide](/guides/electrical-wiring-quotation-malaysia)).
Carpentry is usually the biggest single line. It's priced per foot run, so 20% more cabinet length means roughly 20%
more carpentry cost. For more on cabinet pricing, see the [kitchen cabinet quotation guide](/guides/kitchen-cabinet-quotation-malaysia).

That's **RM77 per sq ft**, at the low end of mid-range. And it doesn't yet include the kitchen countertop, sanitary
fittings, light fittings or a contingency, which homeowner guides suggest setting at 10–15%. Add those and the job
moves well into the RM90–120 per sq ft band. This is why a per-sq-ft quote made on the spot so often goes wrong.

## How contractors should use per-sq-ft figures

- **As a sanity check, not a price.** Build the quotation by trade, then divide the total by the built-up area. If the
  result is far below the range for that scope, look for missing lines.
- **Itemise every line.** Unit, quantity and rate on each line lets you re-measure fairly and price changes
  consistently. A lump sum invites arguments, and homeowner guides warn against quotes with no breakdown.
- **List the exclusions.** Countertops, sanitary ware, light fittings, appliances and management deposits are the usual
  gaps between what the client thinks is included and what is.
- **Watch markup vs margin.** A 30% markup on cost is only a 23% margin. The
  [markup vs margin guide](/guides/markup-vs-margin-contractors) has conversion tables.
