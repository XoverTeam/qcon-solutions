A variation order (VO) is a signed, written change to the original quotation: work **added**, work **omitted**, or
work **changed**. It states the price of the change, the **revised contract sum** and any **extra time**, and the
client signs it **before** you do the work. Below is a free Excel variation order template for Malaysian renovation
contractors and interior designers, and how to use it so changes don't eat your margin.

## What's in the template

- **VO details**: VO number, date, client, site address, the original quotation number and date, who asked for the change, and why.
- **Description of the change**, in plain words the client will understand.
- **Up to 10 items, each marked Add or Omit.** Omissions are credited automatically and shown in brackets.
- **Net change, previously approved VOs and the revised contract sum**, calculated for you.
- **Extra working days and payment terms** for this variation.
- **An approval block** that writes out the new contract sum and the time extension in words, with a line for the client's signature.
- **A VO Register sheet** that logs every variation on the job, with the approved total and the current contract sum.

## Why "add first, settle later" costs you money

Changes are normal in renovation: the client sees the tiles laid and wants bigger ones, the hacking uncovers a
leaking pipe, or the designer moves a socket. The problem isn't the change. It's agreeing on the price **after** the
work is done, when you have no leverage left. A signed VO before the work:

- **fixes the price** while the client still has a choice;
- **records the extra time**, so a later handover isn't counted against you;
- **keeps the contract sum current**, so the final bill has no surprises for either side.

CIDB's standard Contract for Renovation and Small Projects follows the same principle: instructions must be dated
and in writing, and varied work is valued at the contract rates, or at fair market rates where there is no contract rate.

## How to fill in a variation order

1. **Number it and link it to the quotation.** VO-01, VO-02 and so on, with the original quote number and date, so
   anyone can trace the change back.
2. **Describe the change in one or two sentences.** "Replace the 600 × 600mm living room tiles with 600 × 1200mm tiles,
   and add 4 sockets in the kitchen" is better than "tiles change".
3. **List each item as Add or Omit.** A substitution is two lines: omit what you quoted, add what replaces it.
4. **Use your quoted rates.** Credit omissions at the original rate. Price added work at the rate already in your quotation
   if the item is the same. That's the fair, consistent way, and the one clients accept most easily.
5. **State the extra days.** Even "0 days" is worth writing down.
6. **Agree how it will be paid.** Next progress payment, 50/50, or upfront for large custom items.
7. **Get it signed before you start.** A signed PDF or a clear written acceptance in WhatsApp, referring to the VO
   number and amount, is your record.

## Example: changing the floor tiles

The client approved a quotation of **RM85,000**. During the job, they want larger tiles in the living room and four
extra sockets in the kitchen:

| Item | Add / Omit | Unit | Qty | Rate (RM) | Amount (RM) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supply & lay 600 × 1200mm porcelain floor tiles (living room) | Add | sq ft | 320 | 32.00 | 10,240.00 |
| Supply & lay 600 × 600mm homogeneous tiles (living room), as quoted | Omit | sq ft | 320 | 25.00 | (8,000.00) |
| Additional 13A socket outlets, kitchen | Add | point | 4 | 120.00 | 480.00 |
| **Net change** | | | | | **2,720.00** |

The revised contract sum is **RM87,720**, plus three working days. The figures are illustrative. Use your own rates.

Without the omission line, the client might reasonably feel they paid twice for the same floor. With it, the extra
cost is clear and easy to accept.

## Common variation order mistakes

- **Starting the work before the VO is signed**, then negotiating the price afterwards.
- **Forgetting the omission credit** on a substitution, which makes the change look more expensive than it is.
- **Not updating the contract sum**, so the final invoice surprises the client.
- **Lumping several changes into one figure** with no breakdown.
- **Ignoring time.** Extra work takes extra days; write them down.
